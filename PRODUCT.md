# Product

## Register

product

## Users

New Zealand Year 13 students in their final year of secondary school, considering applying to Australian universities. They arrive after mock results drop, carrying a loose mental model of their likely grades. The mood is curiosity, not stress: "I think I'll get Merit in this, Excellence in that; what does that translate to?" They're exploring possibilities, not filing an application. Most are using the tool on a laptop at home or a school computer during a free period.

## Product Purpose

Translate NCEA achievement standards and grades into an estimated Australian Tertiary Admission Rank. The tool lets students search for their standards, assign expected grades, and see where they'd land on the ATAR scale using historical NZQA weighting and distribution data. Success is a student leaving with a clear, trustworthy number and the context to understand what it means, without needing to cross-reference three government PDFs.

## Brand Personality

Precise, friendly, academic.

The voice is that of a sharp, approachable tutor: someone who knows the system inside out but explains it without jargon or condescension. There's a quiet warmth and occasional wit (think Claude.ai's personality: intelligent, slightly playful, never trying too hard). Numbers are presented with confidence and appropriate caveats. The interface respects the student's time and intelligence.

## Anti-references

- **Gamified grade trackers.** No streaks, badges, progress bars for the sake of engagement, or celebratory confetti. The tool is not trying to make studying fun; it's answering a specific question.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards.** No sidebar nav with 12 sections, no settings pages with toggle grids, no onboarding wizards. This is a single-purpose calculator, not a platform.
- **Government forms.** No grey-on-grey inputs, no "Section 4b: Declaration", no walls of legalese before you can do anything. The legal disclaimer exists but doesn't dominate.

## Design Principles

1. **Answer first, explain second.** The ATAR estimate is the headline. Methodology, breakdowns, and caveats are available but never compete with the result.
2. **Respect the question.** Students come with a specific "what if" scenario. The interface should let them build and adjust that scenario with minimal friction, not funnel them through a prescribed flow.
3. **Honest precision.** Show numbers with appropriate confidence. Never overstate accuracy. Present estimates as estimates, not verdicts.
4. **Quiet competence.** The design should feel like it was made by someone who deeply understands the NCEA-to-ATAR pipeline. Confidence expressed through clarity, not decoration.
5. **One tool, done well.** Resist feature creep. Every element earns its place by helping answer the core question.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific WCAG target mandated. Follow sensible defaults: sufficient color contrast for the existing palette, keyboard navigation for core flows, meaningful alt text on the logo and icons.
